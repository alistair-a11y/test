## LinkedIn posts, founder voice

**1. Launch: there's more than one way**

> Most builders I speak to assume a 7-star NatHERS rating (now branded Home Energy Rating) is the only way to meet the NCC's heating and cooling requirement for a new house.
>
> It isn't.
>
> The NCC says its Performance Requirements can be met by a Deemed-to-Satisfy Solution, a Performance Solution, or a combination of both (NCC 2022 Volume Two, A2G1).
>
> For houses, a NatHERS rating is one Deemed-to-Satisfy option. The elemental provisions are another. And H6V2, a Verification Method written into the Code itself, can support a Performance Solution.
>
> Same Code. Same Performance Requirement. A different route.
>
> Over the next few weeks I'll explain how each route works, what it costs, and when NatHERS is still the right call.

**2. Not a loophole**

> "Isn't a Performance Solution just a loophole?"
>
> It's the opposite. A Performance Solution carries more process, not less: a performance-based design brief prepared with the relevant stakeholders, the analysis, an evaluation against agreed acceptance criteria, and a final report (NCC A2G2(4)).
>
> Performance Solutions are used right across the NCC, and every one has to go through this process.
>
> Energy is no different. The question isn't whether it's allowed. It's whether it's done properly.

**3. What's in a star?**

> What does a 7-star NatHERS rating (now branded Home Energy Rating) actually measure?
>
> A modelled estimate of the heating and cooling a home's design would need, per square metre, under standard assumptions about how it's used.
>
> It isn't a meter reading, a power-bill forecast, a measure of your appliances or solar (that's the separate Whole of Home rating), or a check of how the house was built.
>
> The government's own YourHome guide says: "The actual amount of energy used by the building, including for heating and cooling, will be influenced by the behaviour of the occupants, quality of construction and maintenance of appliances, in addition to the modelled thermal performance of the building." [Check the wording on the live page.]
>
> And 7 stars isn't a gold standard. It's the minimum the NatHERS route accepts.
>
> None of that makes NatHERS bad. It makes it a tool, not a verdict.

**4. How H6V2 works, in three steps**

> 1. We model your house as designed.
> 2. We model a reference version of the same house, built to the NCC's own Deemed-to-Satisfy specifications.
> 3. If your design needs no more heating and no more cooling than the reference, it meets the Performance Requirement (H6P1).
>
> Same floor plan, same window sizes and positions, same thermostat settings and occupancy. What can differ is the insulation, the glazing performance and the shading.
>
> That's why it can cost less: you upgrade what your design needs, not what a star target demands.

## Carousel: Myth vs Code

1. Three myths about energy compliance for new WA homes
2. Myth: "NatHERS 7 stars is the only way." Code: Performance Requirements can be met by a Deemed-to-Satisfy Solution, a Performance Solution, or both (A2G1).
3. Myth: "7 stars is the gold standard." Fact: 7 stars is the minimum for the NatHERS option, and the ABCB's handbook says "all compliance options aim to offer an equivalent level of performance".
4. Myth: "A Performance Solution is a loophole." Code: it needs a design brief, analysis, evaluation and a final report (A2G2(4)).
5. So which route costs least? It depends on the design. That's the point.
6. Free Pathway Check: send your plans and we'll tell you which route is likely cheapest, even when it's NatHERS.
7. Sources: NCC 2022 Volume Two A2G1 and A2G2; ABCB Housing energy efficiency handbook (2024).
