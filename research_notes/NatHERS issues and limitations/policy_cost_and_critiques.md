## 1. Cost-effectiveness of 7 stars: the RIS, industry estimates, diminishing returns, affordability, the 2029 pause and the 2026 political debate

### Takeaway
The official case for 7 stars is positive but thin. The ABCB's 2022 package (a 7-star shell plus a whole-of-home budget) reports national average benefit-cost ratios (BCRs) of 1.4 for houses and 1.3 for apartments, with about $185 a year in bill savings per house. This came after the 2021 Consultation RIS had found that costs would exceed benefits. WA-specific BCRs could not be retrieved. Per-home cost estimates run from under $2,500 (the regulators' modelling, as cited by the GBCA) to $7,000+ for Perth (HIA), and up to $33,000 for all of the 2022 and 2025 NCC changes combined (HIA). In August and September 2026 the federal Opposition (Andrew Bragg) proposed a "Basic Australian Standard" that would make energy efficiency an "optional extra". In that fight, a firm selling another way to meet the same standard has more to lose than gain by attacking the standard itself.

### Cited Findings

#### C1. The official benefit case is real but modest (national averages only)

- **Evidence:** ABCB, *NCC 2022 Residential Energy Efficiency: Overview of Provisions* (2022; government): "average energy bill savings approximately $185 per annum for houses and average benefit-cost ratios of 1.4 for new houses and 1.3 for new apartments" (search extract). The figures are presented as net benefits for new households. [ABCB Final Decision RIS page](https://www.abcb.gov.au/ncc-2022-residential-energy-efficiency-final-decision-ris)
- Building Ministers agreed in August 2022 to include the provisions in NCC 2022 (search extract). The search summary did not say which page carried the quote. Likely sources: [Domain](https://www.domain.com.au/advice/what-is-a-7-star-house-and-are-they-worth-more-1165241/) or [Property Council](https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/property-australia/seven-stars-meets-the-market)
- **WA:** ACIL Allen's 2021 Consultation RIS has a table with a Perth row for Class 1 and Class 2, but the extract did not label the metric. #### C2. The 2021 Consultation RIS found costs would exceed benefits; the 2022 Decision RIS reversed this
- **What it is:** The government's own first-round analysis did not support the change.
- **Evidence:** HIA's 2021 submission (industry body) says: "The CRIS concluded that the anticipated costs of the two scenarios would exceed benefits" (search extract). [HIA submission, 2021](https://hia.com.au/our-industry/-/media/files/newsroom/submissions/2021/submission-consultation-ris-energy-efficiency.pdf)
- ACIL Allen (consultant to the ABCB) attributed the low BCRs partly to valuing energy savings at resource cost (wholesale prices and avoided network investment) rather than retail prices, and partly to high household capital costs for the new targets (search extract, paraphrased). [ACIL Allen CRIS 2021](https://acilallen.com.au/uploads/projects/377/ACILAllen_RISProposedNCC2022_2021.pdf)
- A Decision RIS extract shows BCRs of 0.87 and 0.84, but the snippet does not tie them to a scenario. They are unverified and should not be cited until checked. [ABCB Decision RIS final](https://www.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2025/Energy%202022%20RIS%20-%20Decision%20RIS%20final.pdf)
- **Strength:** Moderate. The CRIS finding is confirmed through HIA's quotation of it; the Decision RIS sub-results are unverified.
- **Counter-evidence:** The final Decision RIS reports national BCRs above 1 (1.4 for houses; see C1). Building Ministers adopted that analysis.

#### C3. Disputes over the RIS method, from both sides
- **Industry side:** HIA argued that costs would be "far in excess of regulators' estimates". It said the CRIS costs "were prepared prior to the current increases in building materials due to supply chain constraints". HIA's own table reduced the modelled benefits by adjusting for realised savings, removing household energy-saving benefits and removing health benefits, based on a literature review (search extract; industry body, 2021). [HIA 2021](https://hia.com.au/our-industry/-/media/files/newsroom/submissions/2021/submission-consultation-ris-energy-efficiency.pdf)
- **Discount rate:** "OBPR requires that a central discount rate of 7 per cent is used", with a sensitivity test lowering it to 5.4% from year 30 onwards to reflect updated advice on long-term environmental benefits (search extract; government). [Decision RIS appendices](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Energy%20Efficiency%202022%20Decision%20RIS_appendices_Publication%20version%20(359158.2).pdf)
- **Health benefits:** The Decision RIS includes health benefits from reduced gas and wood use. ACIL Allen notes these benefits "are less material when moving from the current stringency of provisions in the NCC to those proposed for NCC 2022" (search extract). [ACIL Allen CRIS 2021](https://acilallen.com.au/uploads/projects/377/ACILAllen_RISProposedNCC2022_2021.pdf)
- **Researcher side (standards too weak):** A peer-reviewed Energy Policy paper (2019) is titled "Aiming for mediocrity: The case of Australian housing thermal performance". Only the title and the C4/D6 statistic were seen in this session. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421519303878)
- **Advocacy side:** In 2022 the Climate Council called for a higher minimum rating to cut emissions and power bills (headline only). [ABC, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-04-21/energy-efficient-7-star-homes-power-climate-council-report/101000828)
- **Strength:** Moderate. These are contested assumptions, not proven errors.
- **Counter-evidence:** The 7% central rate is a conservative, mandated convention. It tends to reduce the present value of long-lived energy savings, which cuts against the claim that benefits were inflated (inference).

### Inferences
- **Illustrative simple paybacks** (undiscounted and not like-for-like; my arithmetic from the cited figures):
  - HIA Perth: $7,179 ÷ $310 a year ≈ 23 years.
  - Graham/AGWA Perth: $4,262 ÷ $310 a year ≈ 14 years.
  - GBCA's "under $2,500" ÷ ABCB's $185 a year ≈ 13.5 years.
  - The payback "story" depends almost entirely on which cost estimate is used.
- The reversal from the CRIS to the Decision RIS is the strongest "official" cost critique, but it cuts both ways. The adopted analysis found a net benefit, so "the government's own analysis shows 7 stars doesn't pay" would be misleading.
- **Strategic:** H6V2 must meet the same H6P1 performance requirement as the 7-star route (inference; verify against the NCC 2022 H6P1/H6V2 text). Thermarate's commercial interest is therefore in the standard staying mandatory while the route to it stays flexible. Arguments that 7 stars is not worth it, and the Coalition's optional-energy-efficiency proposal, would shrink demand for any compliance service, H6V2 included. The mid-2029 pause gives a stable market.

## 2. Design and construction critiques: orientation, glazing, lightweight versus heavyweight, raised floors, "designing to the software", tropical climates and thermal bridging

### Takeaway
Long-standing academic critiques hold that NatHERS's fixed thermostat and occupancy settings disadvantage some climate-responsive designs, both heavyweight (rammed earth) and lightweight tropical houses. CSIRO replies that natural-ventilation cooling has been modelled since 2003 and may even be over-credited, and it is running a Darwin "Living Lab" to test tropical fit. Cooling-dominated northern zones (the NT and parts of northern WA and Queensland) were carved out of the load limits from the start. CSIRO's thermal-bridging work found steel frames lose about 0.2 to 0.4 stars in a sample house. Certificate data show that about 82% of houses in the 6-star era clustered at the minimum. Almost every design critique comes from the simulation method, which an H6V2 Performance Solution shares.

### Cited Findings

#### D1. Fixed settings disadvantage climate-responsive designs, both heavyweight and lightweight
- **What it is:** Standard thermostat bands and occupancy schedules are said to under-rate designs that rely on occupant-managed comfort, thermal mass or ventilation.
- **Evidence:** An IBPSA Building Simulation 2019 paper, "An Atlas for Rammed Earth Building Thermal Performance in Australia Climates" (peer-reviewed conference), says: "by using fixed heating and cooling thermostat settings, NatHERS energy rating discriminates against the application of climate responsive house construction types, including lightweight tropical houses" (search extract). It cites Soebarto (2000) and Williamson et al. (2010). A second extract of the same paper says it applies to heavyweight rammed earth as well. [IBPSA 2019](https://publications.ibpsa.org/proceedings/bs/2019/papers/BS2019_210313.pdf)
- An NT report hosted by the Environment Centre NT (c. 2020; an NGO-hosted document of unclear authorship) makes two points (search extract, paraphrased): [ECNT-hosted PDF](https://d3n8a8pro7vhmx.cloudfront.net/ecnt/pages/421/attachments/original/1605584587/NATHERS_NT_Final_ANSNAC_15295_.pdf?1605584587=)
  - If NatHERS ratings for traditional hot-climate design are considered inappropriate, this "may not reflect a flaw in the benchmark engine but an unintended consequence of the scheme's settings".
  - NatHERS "assumes that the thermostat setting for cooling in bedrooms is the same as in living rooms".
- **Strength:** Moderate. This is a long-standing academic critique, but it was seen second-hand; Williamson et al. (2010) and Soebarto (2000) were not retrieved.
- **Counter-evidence:** CSIRO said in December 2025 that NatHERS has accounted for the cooling effect of natural ventilation since 2003 and the second-generation Chenath engine. It said NatHERS settings may *overestimate* that effect because they assume occupants adjust openings almost perfectly. CSIRO is involved in the Darwin Living Lab project to make sure the tools suit tropical designs (search extract; government research agency). [CSIRO AHD, Dec 2025](https://ahd.csiro.au/nationwide-house-energy-rating-scheme-nathers-tools-update-december-2025/)

#### D2. Natural ventilation versus real air-conditioner use (Queensland)
- **Evidence:** Mason and Leardini (SSRN preprint, c. 2023), comparing NatHERS with Passive House for a sub-tropical Queensland dwelling, found that natural ventilation "remains the primary method of cooling considered in NatHERS, even though more than 74% of houses in Queensland have some form of active cooling" (search extract). [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4588670)
- **Strength:** Moderate to weak (a preprint).
- **Note:** This critique runs *opposite* to D1, saying NatHERS may be too generous to ventilated designs. That makes "NatHERS penalises tropical design" contestable.

#### D3. Tropical and hot-humid regions, and the NT's lower standard
- The 2019 Decision RIS on heating and cooling load limits proposed excluding the NT and certain northern WA and Queensland climate zones, because those zones normally experience only cooling loads (search extract; government). [OIA, 2019 load-limits Decision RIS](https://oia.pmc.gov.au/sites/default/files/posts/2019/02/2_heating_and_cooling_load_limits_decision_ris_in_pdf.pdf)
- The ABCB standard states: "heating and cooling load limits do not apply in NSW, NT, Tas and parts of Qld and WA" (search extract). [ABCB Standard: NatHERS heating and cooling load limits](https://www.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/ABCB-Standard-NatHERS-heating-cooling-load-limits.pdf); [2022 preview](https://www.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/nathers-heating-cooling-load-limits-Preview-2022.pdf)
- One source says the NT "opted out" of the NCC changes, which is weak; the likely source is [Transformed](https://www.transformed.com.au/nathers/changes-to-energy-efficiency-ratings/). A 2026 glazing-vendor guide says every mainland state and territory except the NT has adopted the requirements (commercial). [APS Double Glazing](https://apsdoubleglazing.com.au/blog/ncc-7-star-energy-rating-explained-2026-guide/)
- Soebarto's 1999 PLEA paper describes typical tropical passive design as a lightweight building with many north and south openings for continuous ventilation, shaded by wide overhangs (search extract). [AIVC record (likely the same paper)](https://www.aivc.org/resource/new-approach-passive-design-residential-buildings-tropical-climate)
- **Strength:** Moderate.
- **Counter-evidence:** CSIRO's Darwin Living Lab and its ventilation modelling (see D1).

#### D4. Thermal bridging penalises steel frames
- **Evidence:** CSIRO, *Thermal Bridging for Residential Building Energy Rating*. The NatHERS site dates it 2020; the PDF was posted in August 2021 (government-commissioned research). NASH (the National Association of Steel-framed Housing) was among the stakeholders consulted. Adding thermal bridging lowered star ratings in most climate zones, especially for metal-framed construction, with an **average drop of about 0.2 to 0.4 stars** for a sample house (search extract, paraphrased). [CSIRO thermal bridging report](https://www.homeenergyrating.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-08/nathers_2022_thermal_bridging_report.pdf)
- Under NCC 2022, thermal-bridging modelling is required only for **repeating steel-framed elements**; timber framing is not affected. The ABCB's aim is "to make thermally bridged metal framed dwellings achieve a similar performance to thermally bridged timber framed dwellings" (search extract; government). [NatHERS thermal bridging page](https://www.nathers.gov.au/thermal-bridging); [NatHERS Technical Note chapter, Dec 2023](https://www.nathers.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-12/NatHERS%20Thermal%20Bridging%20chapter%2020231212.pdf)
- A manufacturer's guide (Kingspan; industry) says: "the effects of thermal bridging are accounted for in NatHERS software, so there are no additional thermal bridging requirements under this compliance option". It adds that the thermal-break clauses 13.2.3(7) and 13.2.5(5) of the Housing Provisions still apply where relevant (search extract). ## 4. Gaming and misuse: assessor shopping, aggressive defaults, paper-only ratings, and regulator warnings or enforcement

### Takeaway

## 5. The strongest defences of NatHERS

### Takeaway
The best defences are empirical and institutional:
- **Measured data:** higher-star homes use less heating energy. CSIRO's 2013 study covered 414 homes in three climate zones. O'Leary et al. (2016, peer-reviewed) found higher-star Adelaide homes use less heating and cooling energy, though the fit was moderate (r² of 0.32 to 0.55).
- **Data and transparency:** NatHERS has a national dataset of 1.6 million+ certificates, with a public address lookup.
- **Quality control:** assessors are accredited under QA protocols.
- **Evolving science:** natural ventilation has been modelled since 2003, there is a published thermal-bridging method, and CSIRO runs the Darwin Living Lab.
- **Policy legitimacy:** a four-year consultation, broad industry acceptance and a final BCR of 1.4.

There are honest caveats. Cooling energy *rose* in 5-star homes, and the correlation with measured use is moderate. A credible campaign should concede these strengths and position H6V2 as another NCC-recognised way to show the same performance, not as a fix for a broken scheme.

### Cited Findings

#### F1. Measured heating reductions (CSIRO's 2013 evaluation)
- **Evidence:** The government commissioned CSIRO to test whether 5-star houses used less heating and cooling energy than 3.5 to 4-star houses. CSIRO sampled 414 houses in three climate zones over a winter and a summer, re-rated them with AccuRate and matched them to energy bills. Houses built after the 5-star standard "used less heating energy but more cooling energy" than those built before it (search extract; government). [CSIRO 5-star evaluation](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/evaluation-5-star-energy-efficiency-standard-residential-buildings-report.pdf)
- **Strength:** Strong (a large field sample with an official method).
- **Caveat:** Higher cooling energy is itself a possible critique, with relevance for Perth's cooling loads.

#### F2. Star ratings track measured heating and cooling use (peer-reviewed)
- **Evidence:** O'Leary et al. (2016, *Energy and Buildings*; peer-reviewed) compared NatHERS modelling with metered use for two sets of Adelaide houses built about ten years apart. They found that "better insulated houses represented by higher stars under the NatHERS scheme do use less energy for heating and cooling", with r² from 0.32 (heating) to 0.55 (cooling) (search extract). [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778816301621); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299130691_Comparing_the_energy_performance_of_Australian_houses_using_NatHERS_modelling_against_measured_household_energy_consumption_for_heating_and_cooling)
- **Strength:** Strong for direction. The fit is moderate, and it covers Adelaide only.

#### F3. A large, transparent national dataset
- **Evidence:** CSIRO holds more than 1.6 million NatHERS ratings and is working to obtain electricity and gas meter data to test ratings against real consumption. It notes that field evaluation needs very large samples to smooth out differences in occupant behaviour (search extract; government, Dec 2025). [CSIRO AHD update](https://ahd.csiro.au/nationwide-house-energy-rating-scheme-nathers-tools-update-december-2025/)
- The AHD hosts an "Energy Rating Finder" that searches certificate headline data by address. [AHD Energy Rating Finder](https://ahd.csiro.au/nathers-certificate-headline-data-address-search/); [CSIRO Data Access Portal](https://data.csiro.au/collection/csiro:74070); [AHD heating and cooling dashboard](https://ahd.csiro.au/dashboards/appliances/heating-and-cooling/)
- **Strength:** Strong.

#### F4. Consistency and quality assurance
- **Evidence:** The AAO protocol sets out QA reviews and remedial action, and NatHERS publishes a Compliance and Enforcement Framework (see G1). [AAO Protocol, 2021](https://www.homeenergyrating.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-10/Assessor%20Accrediting%20Organisations%20Protocol%20v2.0%20_%20October%202021.pdf); [NatHERS Assessor Toolkit](https://www.nathers.gov.au/Assessor-Toolkit)
- **Strength:** Moderate. The framework exists, but no outcome data was found.

#### F5. Science that keeps updating
- **Evidence:**
  - Natural-ventilation cooling has been modelled since 2003, and CSIRO's Darwin Living Lab tests tropical fit (see D1). [CSIRO AHD, Dec 2025](https://ahd.csiro.au/nationwide-house-energy-rating-scheme-nathers-tools-update-december-2025/)
  - The thermal-bridging method is published and aims for timber-steel parity (see D4). [NatHERS thermal bridging](https://www.nathers.gov.au/thermal-bridging)
  - A 2025 tools update (December 2025). [CSIRO AHD](https://ahd.csiro.au/nationwide-house-energy-rating-scheme-nathers-tools-update-december-2025/)
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong.

#### F6. Policy legitimacy and stakeholder acceptance
- **Evidence:**
  - Domain reported a four-year national consultation, with most property-industry bodies backing 7 stars. [Domain](https://www.domain.com.au/advice/what-is-a-7-star-house-and-are-they-worth-more-1165241/)
  - The Property Council called it a "modest" step. [Property Council](https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/property-australia/seven-stars-meets-the-market)
  - The final RIS found a BCR of 1.4 for houses (see C1).
  - Advocates such as the Climate Council pushed for higher standards. [ABC, 2022](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-04-21/energy-efficient-7-star-homes-power-climate-council-report/101000828)
- **Strength:** Moderate.

#### F7. Untested evaluation
- A Victorian government evaluation of the 6-star standard exists, though its date was not confirmed ([Evaluation of Victorian 6 Star Standard, Final Report](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/592010/Evaluation-of-Victorian-6-Star-Standard-Final-Report.pdf)). Its findings were not extracted. See Gaps.

### Inferences
- **NatHERS's credibility rests on data and process. H6V2 shares the science but not the system.** H6V2 has no equivalent public dataset, certificate registry or AAO QA (inference; verify). A campaign that attacks NatHERS's accuracy invites the question "and who checks yours?"
- **Recommended framing** (inference): "NatHERS is a solid national benchmark. H6V2 is a separate, NCC-recognised way to show your home meets the same performance requirement, and for some designs it can find a lower-cost compliant specification."
- The F1 caveat (more cooling energy in 5-star homes) is the one defence-side finding that could support a nuanced critique for Perth. But it is 2013 data under the 5-star regime, so label it with the year.
